# Provenance and Graph Models

Project Knowledge began with a loose “knowledge graph” hypothesis. The corpus showed that graph relationships alone are insufficient, but standards research also shows that we should not invent provenance and relationship semantics from scratch.

This assessment separates **provenance semantics** from **graph representation**.

## W3C PROV

W3C PROV is a family of standards for representing and exchanging provenance across systems and domains. PROV-O expresses the model as an ontology, but its conceptual vocabulary is useful independently of any decision to use RDF/OWL.

### Starting-point concepts

PROV begins with three central concepts:

- **Entity** — a physical, digital, conceptual, or other thing;
- **Activity** — something occurring over time that uses, transforms, or generates entities; and
- **Agent** — something bearing responsibility for an activity or entity.

Core relationships include:

- `used`;
- `wasGeneratedBy`;
- `wasDerivedFrom`;
- `wasAttributedTo`;
- `wasAssociatedWith`; and
- `wasInformedBy`.

Expanded terms include concepts highly relevant to the Project Knowledge corpus:

- `wasRevisionOf`;
- `hadPrimarySource`;
- `specializationOf`;
- `alternateOf`;
- generation/invalidation time; and
- qualified relationships that can themselves carry additional detail.

### Direct fit to observed failures

PROV's vocabulary addresses much of `O-005` and `FM-010`, which warned against collapsing all provenance into a generic `source` field.

For example, the following are meaningfully different:

```text
Artifact B was derived from Artifact A
Artifact B was generated by Activity X
Activity X used Artifact A
Artifact B was attributed to Agent Y
Artifact C was a revision of Artifact B
Artifact D is an alternate representation of Artifact B
```

Those distinctions are much richer than:

```yaml
source: Artifact A
```

### Progressive structure

A particularly strong alignment with Project Knowledge is that PROV explicitly presents a small starting vocabulary and then richer expanded/qualified terms.

That provides a mature precedent for:

```text
simple provenance first
        ↓
qualified detail when valuable
```

rather than requiring every captured object to carry a complete provenance graph.

### What PROV does not solve

PROV is not a complete engineering-knowledge ontology.

It does not, by itself, define Project Knowledge semantics for:

- requirement authority;
- architectural decision state;
- claim/evidence validity;
- contradicts/supports relationships;
- epistemic confidence;
- current-vs-historical authority resolution; or
- pedagogical narrative.

Those concerns can potentially specialize or coexist with PROV rather than forcing PROV terms to mean more than intended.

## RDF-style graph representation

RDF provides a graph-oriented data model where identified resources can be related through predicates.

This is naturally strong for:

- many-to-many relationships;
- traversal from one engineering object to related objects;
- shared identifiers;
- cross-domain vocabularies; and
- integration of heterogeneous information.

That makes graph representation attractive for `UJ-003`, `UJ-006`, `UJ-010`, and `UJ-013`.

## Why a graph is still not the solution

The corpus already showed that relationship structure is only one independent dimension.

A graph containing:

```text
A → B
```

still needs answers to:

- what does the edge mean?
- who asserted it?
- under what authority?
- during what valid interval?
- is it current, historical, disputed, inferred, or generated?
- what source state was it derived from?
- what evidence supports the assertion?
- should a human see it in the current-state view, history view, impact view, or learning narrative?

Graph storage does not remove the need for a careful domain model.

## Semantic identity caution

Global identifiers are useful only if their identity rules are clear.

`PKC-0010` shows that a stable semantic identity can survive path relocation. A future graph model could represent that identity, but assigning an IRI/UUID does not by itself define whether two files, issues, revisions, or projections are:

- the same semantic object;
- revisions of one object;
- alternate representations;
- specializations; or
- separate related objects.

Identity semantics must precede identifier technology.

## Provisional reuse direction

Strong candidates for conceptual reuse:

1. PROV's Entity / Activity / Agent distinction;
2. explicit derivation, usage, generation, attribution, revision, primary-source, alternate, and specialization relationships;
3. qualified relationships when relationship metadata matters;
4. progressive provenance detail; and
5. graph traversal as a useful projection/query capability for many-to-many relationships.

Still undecided:

- RDF versus another graph representation;
- whether a graph is canonical or derived;
- whether provenance records live in source files, an index, or a database;
- identifier formats; and
- how engineering-specific semantics extend or sit beside PROV.

## Key discovery conclusion

The existing standards materially reduce the novelty of the Project Knowledge problem.

We do **not** need to invent a generic provenance model or generic graph model.

The remaining work is to determine the **engineering-specific semantic layer and composition rules** that connect provenance, time, authority, evidence, project artifacts, and human views.
