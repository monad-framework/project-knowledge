# Provisional Terminology

These terms give the project a shared vocabulary during inception. They are deliberately provisional; discovery and domain modeling may rename, split, merge, or reject them.

## Project knowledge

Durable information that helps explain a project's intent, state, structure, history, reasoning, evidence, work, or outcomes.

## Project memory

The preserved body of project knowledge from which people or tools can recover context about the project.

Project memory includes more than the current authoritative state; it may also include historical states, superseded reasoning, and the path by which the project evolved.

## Knowledge object

A provisional term for an individually addressable unit of project knowledge, such as a question, requirement, observation, decision, experiment, finding, concept, artifact, or lesson.

This term does not imply that the final implementation will use object-oriented storage or any specific schema.

## Relationship

A meaningful connection between knowledge objects or between project knowledge and external artifacts.

Examples might eventually include `supports`, `contradicts`, `motivates`, `supersedes`, `implements`, `verified_by`, or `derived_from`, but no canonical relationship vocabulary has yet been established.

## Provenance

Information about where a piece of project knowledge came from: for example a source, observation, experiment, discussion, artifact, person, or prior knowledge object.

## Epistemic state

The status of information with respect to what is known or believed. A question, hypothesis, observation, accepted decision, disputed claim, and verified finding do not carry the same epistemic meaning.

The final state model is not yet defined.

## Temporal state

The relationship of knowledge to time, including when it was introduced, when it was considered valid, when it changed, and whether it was superseded.

## Current state

The subset or projection of project knowledge considered authoritative or operative now.

## Historical state

A representation of what the project knew, believed, intended, or had decided at an earlier point, even where that state is no longer current.

## Projection

A human- or machine-oriented view over shared project knowledge for a particular purpose.

Possible projections include a timeline, architecture view, subsystem page, decision history, learning path, current-state reference, or work-oriented view.

A projection is not assumed to be automatically generated; it may be generated, curated, or hybrid.

## Canonical knowledge

A provisional term for the durable underlying project representation from which one or more projections can be derived or maintained.

The existence and exact shape of a single canonical representation remain hypotheses to validate.

## Capture

The act of introducing potentially useful information into project memory.

Capture may precede complete classification, validation, or relationship modeling.

## Enrichment

Adding structure, relationships, provenance, status, or other context to previously captured information.

## Orientation

A person's working understanding of where a project stands: what it is, how its important pieces fit together, why significant choices were made, what changed, and what matters next.

Recovering orientation after context loss is a central use case for the project.

## Engineering record

The combined durable record of engineering intent, reasoning, decisions, evidence, implementation, validation, and evolution.

## Build in public

A publication practice in which meaningful parts of the engineering process are made visible to others. Project Knowledge may support this practice, but public visibility is not required for the underlying project-memory model.
